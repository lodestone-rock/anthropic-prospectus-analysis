Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures about Market Risk

We are exposed to market risks in the ordinary course of our business. Market risk represents the risk of loss

that may impact our financial position due to adverse changes in financial market prices and rates. Our market

risk exposure is primarily the result of fluctuations in interest rates and foreign currency exchange rates.

Interest Rate Risk

As of June30, 2026, we had $112.4 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $19.3 billion of short-term

investments in a variety of securities, including money market funds, U.S. Treasury and agency securities,

corporate debt securities, and commercial paper. In addition, we had $2.5 billion of restricted cash primarily

due to amounts held as collateral for our lease and other obligations. Our cash, cash equivalents, and short-

term investments are held for working capital purposes, and we do not enter into investments for trading or

speculative purposes. Due to the relatively short-term nature of our investment portfolio, a hypothetical 100

basis point increase or decrease in interest rates would not have had a material effect on the fair value of our

investment portfolio as of June30, 2026.

Foreign Currency Exchange Risk

We transact business globally in multiple currencies, with the vast majority of our revenue denominated in U.S.

dollars and, to a lesser extent, other currencies such as euros and British pounds. Our international revenue, as

well as costs and expenses denominated in foreign currencies, and intercompany transactions with our foreign

subsidiaries, expose us to the risk of fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates against the U.S. dollar. A

hypothetical 10% change in foreign currency exchange rates applicable to our business would not have a

material impact on our consolidated financial statements as of June30, 2026. We have not entered into

derivative or hedging transactions, but we may enter into such arrangements in the future if our exposure to

foreign currency becomes more significant.

Our board of directors determined the fair value of our common stock by first determining the enterprise value

of our business and then allocating the value among the various classes of our securities to derive a per share

closest round of equity financing to the date of the valuation.

In allocating the enterprise value of our business among the various classes of stock prior to this offering, we

used a hybrid method based on a contingent claims analysis (CCA) and a common stock equivalent (CSE)

method. The CCA is based on the Black-Scholes-Merton option pricing model, which models each class of stock

as a call option with a unique claim on our assets. The CSE method presumes that all shares of redeemable

convertible preferred stock convert into common stock based upon their conversion terms and other differences

in the rights and preferences of the shares of redeemable convertible preferred stock are ignored.

After the allocation of enterprise value to the various classes of stock, a discount for lack of marketability

(DLOM) is applied to arrive at a fair value of the common stock. A DLOM is meant to account for the lack of

opportunity to sell a private company stock that is not traded on public exchanges. In making the final

determination of common stock value, consideration is also given to the recent sales andor expected sales of

common stock.

subjective, such as those regarding our expected future revenue, expenses, and future cash flows, discount

Fair Value of Convertible Notes, Derivative Liabilities, and SAFEs

We elected to account for our convertible notes at fair value pursuant to Accounting Standards Codification

(ASC) Topic 825, Financial Instruments. The convertible notes are recognized at their estimated fair value on

our consolidated balance sheet, and changes in fair value are recorded in change in fair value of convertible

notes, derivative liabilities, and SAFEs in our consolidated statement of operations. We elected the fair value

option to eliminate the accounting complexity that would otherwise result from separately accounting for the

liability and equity components of the convertible notes under the traditional bifurcation model.

In connection with the 2023 and 2024 note purchase agreements with both Amazon and Google, we recognized

derivative liabilities representing obligations to issue future convertible notes pursuant to the terms of those

agreements. The derivative liabilities were measured at estimated fair value, with changes in fair value recorded

in change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities, and SAFEs in the consolidated statement of

operations.

The estimated fair value of the convertible promissory notes is determined using a hybrid approach comprising

a CCA and a CSE method. The analysis estimates the fair value of the convertible promissory notes using a

scenario-based analysis that considers the probability-weighted present value of the instruments across the

range of possible outcomes available to the note holders, including conversion into redeemable convertible

preferred stock, Class N common stock, cash repayment at maturity, and cash settlement at a premium upon a

change of control. The scenarios included consider the probability-weighted value of the instruments in the

CCA and CSE framework.

The application of this methodology involves the use of complex and subjective estimates, judgments, and

assumptions, including the probability and timing of each settlement scenario such as a qualified equity

financing, initial public offering, direct listing, or change of control the estimated fair value of our equity

Income Taxes

We account for income taxes using the asset and liability method, recognizing deferred tax assets and liabilities

for temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of assets and liabilities, as well as for

net operating loss and credit carryforwards, measured at enacted tax rates applicable in the years of expected

reversals. We establish valuation allowances when necessary to reduce deferred tax assets to amounts more-

likely-than-not expected to be realized, based on the weighting of positive and negative evidence.

Assessing the realizability of our deferred tax assets is an area of significant management judgment. We

evaluate our deferred tax assets for recoverability based on historical taxable income, projected future taxable

income, reversal patterns of existing temporary differences, and available tax planning strategies. As an AI

company operating in a rapidly evolving market with significant ongoing investment in compute and

infrastructure, our projections of future taxable income are inherently uncertain. Should our assessment of the

recoverability of deferred tax assets change, our income tax provision would be adjusted in that period, which

could materially impact our results of operations.

We account for uncertain tax positions in accordance with ASC Topic 740, Income Taxes. Tax benefits from

uncertain positions are recognized when it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on

examination based on its technical merits, measured at the amount that is more likely than not to be realized

upon settlement. These assessments require judgment, particularly with respect to the qualification and

documentation of research and development tax credits, where supporting evidence may be subject to differing

interpretations. The actual outcome of these matters may differ materially from our current estimates. We

recognize interest and penalties related to unrecognized tax benefits within our provision for income taxes.

Loss Contingencies

We are involved in legal claims, proceedings, and other regulatory and administrative matters that arise in the

ordinary course of business, certain of which may include speculative claims for substantial or indeterminate

amounts of damages.

We routinely assess each significant matter and its potential financial exposure. We record a liability when we

believe it is both probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated, and we

disclose matters for which a loss is reasonably possible and the loss or range of loss can be estimated. These

assessments require significant judgment in determining both the likelihood and estimated amount of potential

losses. We base our accruals on the best information available at the time, taking into account the specific facts

and circumstances of each matter, the merits of our defenses, and the impact of negotiations, settlements,

regulatory proceedings, rulings, and advice of legal counsel. Our estimates are subject to change as additional

information becomes available and as matters develop, and we have in prior periods revised our assessments

based on updated information.
