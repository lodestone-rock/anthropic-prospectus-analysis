Overview

We have entered into NPAs with each of Google (through Google LLC), Amazon (through Amazon Web

Services, Inc.), and Broadcom that provide for the issuance of convertible promissory notes and, in certain

cases, shares of our non-voting capital stock. Our NPAs with Google consist of agreements entered into in

September 2023 and August 2024, pursuant to which all convertible promissory notes previously issued

thereunder have been fully converted into non-voting redeemable convertible preferred stock and under which

no additional notes may be issued, and an agreement entered into in March 2026 (Google 2026 NPA), which

provides for potential future financing capacity, subject to certain conditions described below. Our NPAs with

Amazon consist of the Amazon 2023 NPA and the Amazon 2024 NPA, pursuant to which we previously issued

convertible promissory notes, approximately $6.2 billion aggregate principal amount of which remains

outstanding as described below under Convertible NotesOutstanding Notes, and under which no

additional notes may be issued, and the 2026 Stock Purchase and Convertible Note Facility Agreement, dated

as of April 20, 2026, by and between us and Amazon (Amazon 2026 NPA), which provides for potential future

financing capacity, subject to certain conditions described below. The Infrastructure Partner 2026 NPA

provides for potential future financing capacity, if needed, subject to certain conditions described below. The

principal economic terms of the NPAs that relate to our outstanding notes and potential future financing

capacity are summarized in the tables below. To the extent an NPA creates an obligation to issue notes in the

future, that obligation is accounted for as a derivative liability on our consolidated balance sheets until the

related note is issued. For additional information, including the historical issuance and conversion of notes

under these agreements, see Notes 9 and 16 to our consolidated financial statements included elsewhere in this

prospectus.

Operating Activities

Net cash used in operating activities for the year ended December 31, 2025 decreased by $3.1 billion compared

to the year ended December 31, 2024. The decrease was primarily driven by a decrease in prepaid hosting

services reflecting the consumption during 2025 of hosting capacity that had been prepaid in prior periods and

an increase in accounts payable, accrued expenses, and other current liabilities due to increased CSP costs and

the timing of related payments. The decrease was partially offset by higher cash payments, including a payment

related to the Bartz settlement, higher cash compensation costs from headcount growth, and an increase in

accounts receivable.

Net cash used in operating activities for the year ended December 31, 2024 increased by $4.1 billion compared

to the year ended December 31, 2023. The increase was primarily driven by an increase in prepaid hosting

services due to business expansion requiring additional hosting capacity.

Net cash provided by operating activities was $6.6 billion for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 as

compared to cash used in operating activities of $2.0 billion for the nine months ended September 30, 2025.

Our operating cash flows result primarily from cash received from our customers, offset by cash payments we

make for products and services, employee compensation, operating leases, and interest. The increase in

operating cash flow was primarily driven by an improvement in net loss adjusted for non-cash items, partially

offset by a lower benefit from changes in working capital, primarily reflecting the timing of payments to vendors

and cash receipts from customers. Working capital at any point in time is subject to many variables, including

the timing of billings and cash collections, vendor payment terms, and the timing of invoice receipt and

payment.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities for the year ended December 31, 2025 increased by $3.1 billion compared

to the year ended December 31, 2024. The increase was primarily driven by increased purchases of new short-

term investments, including U.S. Treasury and agency securities, and corporate debt securities, as well as

higher capital expenditures related to servers, networking, and infrastructure equipment, and purchases of

intangible assets. These increases were partially offset by proceeds from sales and maturities of short-term

investments.

Net cash used in investing activities for the year ended December 31, 2024 increased by $20 million compared

to the year ended December 31, 2023. The increase was primarily driven by increased capital investments in

leasehold improvements and purchases of intangible assets.

Net cash used in investing activities was $36.5 billion for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 as

compared to cash used in investing activities of $2.0 billion for the nine months ended September 30, 2025,

which primarily reflects the increased purchases of short-term investments, including commercial paper, U.S.

Treasury and agency securities and corporate debt securities, in addition to prepayments for leased assets and

equipment, and higher capital expenditures related to servers, networking, and infrastructure equipment. This

was partially offset by proceeds from sales and maturities of short-term investments.

Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures about Market Risk

We are exposed to market risks in the ordinary course of our business. Market risk represents the risk of loss

that may impact our financial position due to adverse changes in financial market prices and rates. Our market

risk exposure is primarily the result of fluctuations in interest rates and foreign currency exchange rates.

Interest Rate Risk

As of September30, 2026, we had $95.6 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $36.3 billion of short-term

investments in a variety of securities, including money market funds, U.S. Treasury and agency securities,

corporate debt securities, and commercial paper. In addition, we had $2.6 billion of restricted cash primarily

due to amounts held as collateral for our lease and other obligations. Our cash, cash equivalents, and short-

term investments are held for working capital purposes, and we do not enter into investments for trading or

speculative purposes. A hypothetical 100 basis point increase or decrease in interest rates would have resulted

in a decrease or increase, respectively, of $310 million in the market value of our cash equivalents and short-

term investments as of September30, 2026.

Foreign Currency Exchange Risk

We transact business globally in multiple currencies, with the vast majority of our revenue denominated in U.S.

dollars and, to a lesser extent, other currencies such as euros and British pounds. Our international revenue, as

well as costs and expenses denominated in foreign currencies, and intercompany transactions with our foreign

subsidiaries, expose us to the risk of fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates against the U.S. dollar. A

hypothetical 10% change in foreign currency exchange rates applicable to our business would not have had a

material impact on our consolidated financial statements as of September30, 2026. We have not entered into

derivative or hedging transactions, but we may enter into such arrangements in the future if our exposure to

foreign currency becomes more significant.

Stock-Based Compensation

We measure compensation expense for all stock-based payment awards, including stock options and RSUs,

granted to employees, directors, and other service providers, based on the estimated fair value of the awards on

the date of grant. The most significant input in determining the fair value of a stock option is the estimated fair

value of our common stock. The estimated fair value of our common stock is also used to measure the grant-

date fair value of RSUs and RSAs, including those subject to performance-based vesting conditions.

Stock Options

We estimate the fair value of stock options granted to employees, consultants, officers, and directors using the

Black-Scholes-Merton option pricing model on the date of grant. The fair value of stock options that are

expected to vest is recognized as compensation expense on a straight-line basis over the requisite service

period. Forfeitures are accounted for when they occur.

The Black-Scholes-Merton model considers several variables and assumptions in estimating the fair value of

stock-based awards. These variables include

Fair Value of Common Stock. Because our common stock is not yet publicly traded, we must estimate

the fair value of our common stock. Our board of directors considers numerous objective and

subjective factors to determine the fair value of our common stock as discussed in the section titled

Common Stock Valuations below.

Expected Volatility. Since we have no trading history of our common stock, the expected volatility of

our underlying common stock is estimated by taking the weighted-average historical volatility of a

group of comparable publicly traded companies over a period equal to the expected life of the options.

Expected Term. Expected term represents the period over which we anticipate stock-based awards to

be outstanding. We estimate the expected term based on the options vesting term and contractual

terms, such that the expected life is equal to the average of the stock-based awards weighted-average

vesting period and its contractual term.

Risk-Free Rate. The expected risk-free rate assumption is based on the U.S. Treasury instruments

whose term is consistent with the expected term of the stock options.

Expected Dividend Yield. The expected dividend yield is zero, as we have never declared or paid any

dividends and have no current plans to do so.

As of September30, 2026, there was approximately $1.8 billion of total unrecognized compensation cost

Our board of directors determined the fair value of our common stock by first determining the enterprise value

Fair Value of Convertible Notes, Derivative Liabilities, and SAFEs

We elected to account for our convertible notes at fair value pursuant to Accounting Standards Codification

(ASC) Topic 825, Financial Instruments. The convertible notes are recognized at their estimated fair value on

our consolidated balance sheet, and changes in fair value are recorded in change in fair value of convertible

notes, derivative liabilities, and SAFEs in our consolidated statement of operations. We elected the fair value

option to eliminate the accounting complexity that would otherwise result from separately accounting for the

liability and equity components of the convertible notes under the traditional bifurcation model.

In connection with the 2023 and 2024 note purchase agreements with both Amazon and Google, we recognized

derivative liabilities representing obligations to issue future convertible notes pursuant to the terms of those

agreements. The derivative liabilities were measured at estimated fair value, with changes in fair value recorded

in change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities, and SAFEs in the consolidated statement of

operations.

The estimated fair value of the convertible promissory notes is determined using a hybrid approach comprising

a CCA and a CSE method. The analysis estimates the fair value of the convertible promissory notes using a

scenario-based analysis that considers the probability-weighted present value of the instruments across the

range of possible outcomes available to the note holders, including conversion into redeemable convertible

preferred stock or Class N common stock, cash repayment at maturity, and cash settlement at a premium upon

a change of control. The scenarios included consider the probability-weighted value of the instruments in the

CCA and CSE framework.

The application of this methodology involves the use of complex and subjective estimates, judgments, and

assumptions, including the probability and timing of each settlement scenario such as a qualified equity

financing, initial public offering, direct listing, or change of control the estimated fair value of our equity

securities into which the notes may convert the applicable conversion prices, which vary by note series and are

contingent on factors such as elapsed time since issuance and contractual caps and percentage discounts and

the ownership thresholds and rollover provisions that determine the extent to which principal balances convert

into equity versus being reissued as rollover notes. In particular, the ownership cap and rollover mechanism

Changes in any or all of these estimates and assumptions, or the relationships between those assumptions,

Income Taxes

We account for income taxes using the asset and liability method, recognizing deferred tax assets and liabilities

for temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of assets and liabilities, as well as for

net operating loss and credit carryforwards, measured at enacted tax rates applicable in the years of expected

reversals. We establish valuation allowances when necessary to reduce deferred tax assets to amounts more-

likely-than-not expected to be realized, based on the weighting of positive and negative evidence.

Assessing the realizability of our deferred tax assets is an area of significant management judgment. We

evaluate our deferred tax assets for recoverability based on historical taxable income, projected future taxable

income, reversal patterns of existing temporary differences, and available tax planning strategies. As an AI

company operating in a rapidly evolving market with significant ongoing investment in compute and

infrastructure, our projections of future taxable income are inherently uncertain. Should our assessment of the

recoverability of deferred tax assets change, our income tax provision would be adjusted in that period, which

could materially impact our results of operations.

We account for uncertain tax positions in accordance with ASC Topic 740, Income Taxes. Tax benefits from

uncertain positions are recognized when it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on

examination based on its technical merits, measured at the amount that is more likely than not to be realized

upon settlement. These assessments require judgment, particularly with respect to the qualification and

documentation of research and development tax credits, where supporting evidence may be subject to differing

interpretations. The actual outcome of these matters may differ materially from our current estimates. We

recognize interest and penalties related to unrecognized tax benefits within our provision for income taxes.
