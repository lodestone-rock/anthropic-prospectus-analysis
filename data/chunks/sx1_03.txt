Our ability to maintain our position at the frontier of AI depends in part on our use of AI systems to accelerate

our research processes, a recursive dynamic we refer to as closing the loop, in which successive versions of our

models assist in building the next, with the goal of compounding capability gains over time. Our models have

AI systems. If we fail to direct sufficient compute and research efforts to AI-assisted development, or if our

Electrical Power and Fuel Constraints

The operation of data centers at the scale required for frontier AI development and deployment consumes

substantial quantities of electrical power. The availability of sufficient electrical power has emerged as a

States. Electrical grid infrastructure in the United States and other countries was not designed to accommodate

the rapid increase in demand associated with AI development, and the lead times required to build new

generation capacity, transmission infrastructure, and grid interconnections can extend for years. Demand on

industry, and this growing demand is occurring at a time when power generation capacity additions,

transmission expansion, and grid modernization efforts have not kept pace and certain existing generation

timelines in certain regions. We have publicly committed to covering the cost of electricity price increases to
