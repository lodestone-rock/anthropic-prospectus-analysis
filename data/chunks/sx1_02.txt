Select Defined Terms

The following are abbreviations, acronyms, and definitions of certain terms used in this prospectus

agent loop refers to the iterative cycle in which an AI agent receives a task, takes an action, such as planning

a project, running code, or calling a tool, observes the result, and decides its next step, with this cycle repeating

until the task is complete. The agent loop is the core execution pattern underlying agentic AI behavior.

agentic AI refers to AI systems that, with varying degrees of autonomy, can plan, determine, and sequence

actions and execute them to accomplish defined objectives, including through interaction with external tools,

services, datasets, and environments on behalf of a user.

AI refers to artificial intelligence, a machine-based system that learns or is trained from data and, for explicit

or implicit objectives and with varying levels of autonomy, processes that data to infer patterns, generate

outputssuch as predictions, content, recommendations, and decisionsand act upon physical or virtual

environments.

AI accelerators refer to processors purpose-built or adapted for the large-scale computations required to

pre-train, post-train, refine, and run AI models. These processors are significantly more efficient and faster

than general-purpose processors for AI workloads.

API refers to application programming interface, a standardized interface that allows external software to

access a systems capabilities programmatically. AI model providers may offer APIs that allow software

developers to send inputs to an AI model and receive outputs, enabling integration into the software developers

products and workflows.

Applied AI refers to a discipline and organizational function focused on embedding AI capabilities directly

into production business environments to address specific customer requirements. Applied AI teams, which

include forward-deployed engineers, work with enterprises to design and deploy custom solutions, specialized

workflows, and domain-specific implementations tailored to a customers own systems and processes.

closing the loop refers to a development cycle in which successive versions of an AI model assist in building

the next, with the goal of compounding capability gains over time.

compute refers to the computational processing resources used to train and run AI models, including,

without limitation, specialized AI accelerators, memory, networking, storage, and power systems that are

deployed in data centers or other physical computing environments.

compute multipliers refer to algorithmic and methodological improvements in training and inference that

increase the effective output of a given amount of compute.

computer use refers to an AI capability that allows a model to observe and interact with a graphical user

interface, such as clicking, typing, and navigating applications, similar to how a human operator would.

Constitutional AI refers to an approach to training AI models using a set of written principles, which we

refer to as Claudes constitution, currently organized around four priorities being broadly safe, broadly ethical,

compliant with our guidelines, and genuinely helpful. Under Constitutional AI, our models evaluate, critique,

and revise their own responses against these principles, enabling us to define and control model behavior

through auditable principles, rather than relying solely on fixed rule-based instructions or human reviewers.

context window refers to the maximum amount of text, code, or other content, measured in tokens, that an

AI model can process in a given request, encompassing instructions, prior conversation history, user input, and

the models output.

CSP refers to a cloud services provider, a technology company that owns and operates large-scale cloud

computing infrastructure and provides on-demand compute, storage, and networking. CSPs may also serve as

distribution channels through which AI products and services are made available to customers. The largest

frontier AI refers to AI systems at the current leading edge of capability and safety, performing at or near

the highest levels of any existing system across a broad range of tasks.

hallucinations refer to outputs in which an AI model generates factually incorrect or fabricated information

with apparent confidence.

inference refers to the process by which a trained AI model processes an input and generates an output.

Inference occurs each time a user or agent interacts with an AI system.

interleaved thinking refers to a capability whereby an AI model interposes discrete reasoning steps

between successive tool calls within a single response, rather than confining its reasoning to a single planning

phase at the outset.

interpretability refers to research into how and why AI models produce specific outputs, with the goal of

making model behavior more transparent and verifiable, especially in high-stakes applications.

knowledge work refers to complex cognitive work and related professional activities, including obtaining,

producing, analyzing, and communicating information through tasks such as coding, drafting analyses,

synthesizing research, building financial models, and managing and automating documents and workflows.

LLMs refer to large language models, or AI models trained primarily on large datasets to understand and

generate natural language and computer code.

mechanistic interpretability refers to a subfield of interpretability research focused on reverse-

engineering the internal computations of neural networks. Rather than inferring model behavior from inputs

and outputs alone, mechanistic interpretability seeks to identify the specific features and circuits within a model

that give rise to its behavior with the goal of making model reasoning auditable at the mechanism level and

detecting failure modes, including deceptive or unsafe behavior, that may not surface in standard testing.

model weights refer to the numerical parameters encoding a trained AI models behavior, which are the

primary intellectual asset of a frontier AI developer, representing the accumulated investment in compute, data,

and research.

multi-agent orchestration refers to an architecture in which multiple AI agents collaborate on a shared

task, with one coordinating while others execute subtasks in parallel, enabling more complex, long-horizon

work than a single agent can complete alone.

open-weight refers to an AI model whose trained model weights, or the numerical parameters encoding the

models learned behavior, are publicly released, allowing third parties to download, run, and modify the model,

subject to the terms of the models license, without necessarily providing access to the underlying training data

or code.

pay-for-intelligence or consumption refers to our revenue model under which customers pay for their

usage of our intelligence service, which is metered in tokens and priced according to the model selected.

persistent, in the context of AI applications, refers to denoting data, state, or context that is continuously

retained beyond the boundary of a single operation, session, or interaction, remaining accessible for reuse

across subsequent steps, sessions, or workflows without requiring reinitialization.

post-training refers to the collection of techniques applied to a pre-trained model to refine its behavior,

including reinforcement learning, supervised refinement methods, and other alignment methods. Post-training

is the stage in which much of the work to make a frontier model helpful, accurate, and safe is performed,

including the application of safety and alignment techniques.

pre-training refers to the initial stage of training an AI model, in which the model learns general patterns

and representations from large-scale datasets of text, code, and other data, typically by predicting the next

token in a sequence. The output of pre-training is a base model with broad capabilities that has not yet been

adapted for any specific deployment context.

primitives refer to the foundational, modular building blocks of a software system from which more

complex applications and workflows are constructed.

prompt injection refers to an adversarial technique in which malicious instructions are embedded in

content an AI model is asked to process, such as websites, documents, or tool outputs, with the aim of

overriding or manipulating the models intended behavior, typically without the users knowledge.

prosumers refer to individual users who subscribe to AI tools to perform knowledge work.

reinforcement learning refers to a machine learning approach in which a model learns to perform a task

by taking actions, receiving feedback signals called rewards, and adjusting its behavior to maximize the rewards

it accrues over time. In contemporary AI development, reinforcement learning is widely used to refine model

behavior after pre-training, including to improve helpfulness, accuracy, and adherence to safety guidelines.

RLHF refers to reinforcement learning from human feedback, a specific form of reinforcement learning in

which human evaluators rate the models responses, and those ratings are used as the reward signal to guide

training.

Run-Rate Revenue is an operating metric that refers to the estimated annualized value of our revenue-

generating activity as of a measurement date, calculated as the sum of (i) our consumption-based fees during

the trailing 28-day period ended on the measurement date, multiplied by 13, and (ii) our aggregate monthly

subscription fees as of the measurement date, multiplied by 12. For purposes of calculating Run-Rate Revenue,

consumption-based fees and subscription-based fees are derived from customer usage and active subscriptions.

SIs refer to systems integrators, or third-party technology and professional services firms that design, build,

and deploy integrated solutions for customers by combining products and services from multiple technology

vendors into unified systems tailored to customer needs.

tokens refer to numeric units into which an AI model converts the data it processes, including text, images,

audio, and video, the number of which varies by the model and tokenizer applied. Tokens are the smallest

individual units of a language model and can correspond to words, subwords, characters, or bytes. A tokenizer

is the component of a model that converts customer inputs into the sequence of tokens the model processes and

then converts the processed tokens into the outputs provided to customers.

The Importance of Safety and Ethical Behavior

While the potential benefits of AI are extraordinary, its rapid advancement will impact the economy and society

in ways that could create significant disruption, particularly if the technology is not developed and deployed

responsibly. For example, bad actors have already conducted high-profile cyberattacks using AIs capabilities to

discover vulnerabilities, generate malware, and evade detection tools other risks associated with misuse,

including in the domain of biology, could result in even greater harm. AI deployed at scale also has the potential

to concentrate power and wealth both within and between countries in ways that could erode trust, harm

society, and destabilize the geopolitical order. Misaligned AI systems could corrupt institutional decision-

making or cause large-scale harm through autonomous actions that are not visible or understandable to their

users and operators. We believe that frontier AI companies must invest in safety and alignment to help ensure

the responsible diffusion of this technology. We also believe that safety, alignment, and ethical behavior

resonate particularly strongly with enterprises, where rigorous safeguards, predictable model behavior, and

demonstrable accountability have become decisive criteria in selecting an AI partner for mission-critical

workloads.

Public Benefit Corporation Status

Consistent with our long-term commitment to AI safety and responsibility, we are incorporated as a PBC under

Delaware law. PBCs are a class of corporations that are intended to produce a public benefit and operate in a

responsible and sustainable manner. Under Delaware law, a PBC is required to identify in its certificate of

incorporation the public benefit or benefits it will promote, and its directors have a duty to manage the affairs of

the corporation in a manner that balances the pecuniary interests of the corporations stockholders, the best

interests of those materially affected by the corporations conduct, and the specific public benefit purpose

identified in the corporations certificate of incorporation. As provided in our amended and restated certificate

of incorporation, the public benefit that we promote, which we must always consider in the management of our

company, is the Company Purpose.

Long-Term Benefit Trust

In 2023, we implemented an innovative governance structure featuring the LTBT, which is designed to ensure

that we are managed in a manner that responsibly balances our tripartite goals as a PBC the pecuniary

interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the Company

Purpose. The LTBT is intended to operate as an independent body of five financially disinterested voting

trustees (Trustees) with expertise in AI safety, national security, public policy, and social enterprise. Through

a special class of common stock, Class T common stock, the LTBT generally has the authority to elect and

remove the Class T directors, who together will constitute a majority of the authorized members of our board of

directors. Our Class T common stock does not otherwise have voting rights on matters submitted to

stockholders generally.

Board Governance

Decisions regarding our business and affairs are made by our board of directors. Immediately prior to the

completion of this offering, we will enter into a stockholders agreement (the Stockholders Agreement) with

the LTBT and the Founder LLC, under which they will agree to vote their shares and take other actions to give

effect to the board composition, director nomination, and director removal provisions described in our

governing documents. Collectively, the LTBT and the Founder LLC will have the ability to elect all members of

our board of directors. Regardless of the class or classes of stock by which a director is elected, there is no limit

to the number of terms a director may serve.

While the LTBT has significant influence over our board of directors through its ability to generally elect and

remove a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors, neither the LTBT nor the Trustees have

decision-making authority in the day-to-day management or strategy setting of the company. The LTBTs role is

to select directors who are well-equipped to make decisions in furtherance of the responsible balancing of the

pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the

Company Purpose, and to support our managements decision-making through advice and guidance.

The Founder LLC will also have significant influence over our business and affairs through its ability to vote our

Class F common stock, which is entitled to an aggregate number of votes that equals 50.1% of the total voting

power of our outstanding shares of capital stock with respect to matters submitted to stockholders generally,

including the election of our directors other than the Class T directors.
