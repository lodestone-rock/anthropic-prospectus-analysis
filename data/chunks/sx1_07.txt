Our Financial Model

Our financial model is built on four primary drivers that define the trajectory of our results

Adoption of Claude. The advancing intelligence and capabilities of each successive version of a

Claude model drive the diffusion of Claude across the global economy. As new customers adopt Claude

and existing customers expand their usage into new workflows, our revenue grows.

Compute Efficiency. We are focused on increasing the intelligence produced and revenue generated

per dollar of compute invested and have delivered significant efficiency improvements.

Operating Efficiency. Our high talent density, efficient go-to-market, and how extensively our

teams leverage Claude in their work help to drive improved operating margin, Adjusted Operating

Margin, and revenue per employee.

Compute Investments. Investing in compute is critical to support our long-term growth, and we

intend to continue making substantial investments in compute.

The drivers of our financial model materialize on different timelines. The adoption of Claude and associated

revenue growth, compute efficiency, and operating efficiency have each increased rapidly over time. Compute is

procured and committed in advance and the expense is incurred as it is brought online and added to our

fungible pool, often in large, discrete steps. The fungible pool can support all workloads dynamically, including

research, model development, serving customers, and internal usage of Claude. As a result, there is a lag

between when investment is committed and when the full revenue associated with such investment is realized.

This dynamic has produced a financial model in which our operating margin and Adjusted Operating Margin

for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 have expanded significantly year over year. As significant

incremental compute is brought online in future periods, the associated expense will be recognized in advance

of the full revenue that the compute will ultimately support, and we expect operating margin and Adjusted

Operating Margin may compress in those periods. At our current stage, this cycle is an inherent aspect of our

Compute Efficiency

Compute is the most significant operating cost in our business, and compute efficiency is a critical determinant

of our operating efficiency and revenue. Our research-driven algorithmic improvements in training and

inference optimization and multi-chip compute architecture have combined to dramatically increase our

compute efficiency as our revenue has scaled. The result is that we are able to continue increasing the

intelligence our research can produce and the revenue our business can generate per dollar of compute

investment.

We define compute efficiency as maximizing the intelligence produced and revenue generated per dollar of

compute invested. Three pillars drive our compute efficiency

Training Efficiency. Our researchers regularly innovate compute multipliers. Increasingly, Claude

itself assists in the research and engineering that produce each successive version of our models, which

is a recursive dynamic we refer to as closing the loop, with successive versions of our models

accelerating the development of the next and compounding these gains across model generations.

Inference Efficiency. Our researchers have delivered meaningful compute multipliers for inference

reducing the compute required to serve a given level of model performancewhile our engineering

teams have driven significant optimizations in how efficiently that compute is utilized in production.

This allows us to provide greater value for customers and generate greater revenue from our compute

base over time, which is critical as we have consistently operated in a compute-constrained

environment where customer demand exceeds available compute.

Multi-Chip Compute Approach. We train and serve Claude natively across four major chip

families (AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs, and AMD GPUs) using a proprietary

orchestration layer that enables us to optimize workloads for price and performance. This flexibility,

which took years and significant investment to build and we believe is difficult to replicate at scale,

expands our available compute, reduces dependence on any single vendor, and enables us to

continuously route workloads to the most optimal hardware for those workloads. This foundation,

paired with Claudes increasing understanding of chip and model architectures, allows us to onboard

new chip platforms in a fraction of the time this once required.

Compute Investments

Investing in compute is a prerequisite for long-term growth. We invest in compute for research, model

development, serving customers, and internal usage of Claude. Our commercial traction, research progress, and

the scale of the opportunity ahead give us the conviction to continue investing aggressively in compute. An

increasing portion of these investments relates to building out our own compute and infrastructure and

vertically integrating across the compute supply chain to secure long-term compute at the scale required for

frontier model development. These investments take the form of capital expenditures and other commitments,

including lease obligations, that we incur well in advance of the related compute becoming operational and

generating revenue. As a result, we expect this build-out to change the cash flow profile of our business and to

be a significant use of net cash in operating and investing activities for the foreseeable future. See the section

In addition, as our compute needs have grown substantially, we are shifting from sourcing compute solely

through fixed-term cloud agreements to a hybrid model that also includes leasing chips directly and hosting

them in dedicated facilities operated on our behalf. We refer to this additional source of compute as Anthropic

Direct, which helps to address our scaling compute requirements and we believe will provide long-term

economic advantages. Anthropic Direct represents a more flexible use of capital by allowing us to separate our

commitments for data center capacity from our commitments for the compute infrastructure deployed inside

them. Because data centers require longer lead times but represent a smaller fraction of total compute costs,

this approach enables us to secure data center capacity early while deferring larger accelerator investments

until our specific compute needs are more certain.

How We Generate Revenue

At the foundation of our business is our family of four Claude model classesMythos (including Fable), Opus,

Sonnet, and Haikueach optimized for a different point on the capability-cost-speed curve. Our frontier

models allow customers to match the right model to each use case. Customers typically use higher-capability

models for complex reasoning, coding, and agentic workflows where greater model intelligence is required.

Customers also frequently adopt our full range of models, routing individual tasks to the models that are most

optimized to meet their needs along the capability-cost-speed curve. The model a customer uses and the volume

of usage are the primary drivers of customer spend. We establish a direct contractual relationship with the

customer and offer customers the flexibility to purchase directly from us or through CSP marketplaces, and

many customers utilize both channels.

Pay-for-intelligence, our consumption-based revenue model, is our primary revenue model and currently

represents the substantial majority of our total revenue. Under this model, customers pay for usage, which is

metered in tokens and priced according to the model selected. Our pay-for-intelligence revenue is primarily

generated from customers building applications incorporating Claude or utilizing our first-party applications.

In addition, we offer certain of our applications on a subscription basis, serving both enterprises and

prosumers. Under this model, customers pay a subscription fee for a specified level of usage and capabilities

during the subscription term, which is generally 12 months or less.

Depending on the model, platform, or applications chosen by our customers, they enter into pay-for-

intelligence pricing, subscription-based pricing, or both. For example, when subscribers approach or exhaust

the usage capacity included in their tier, they may utilize a higher subscription tier or purchase more

intelligence directly, creating a seamless bridge from subscription to incremental pay-for-intelligence revenue.

A growing number of enterprise customers agree to non-cancelable annual or multi-year contracts with

minimum spend commitments in exchange for a committed price list. These contracts result primarily in

consumption-based revenue.

Consumption-based revenue increased from $288 million, or 75% of total revenue, for the year ended

December 31, 2024, to $3.8 billion, or 83% of total revenue, for the year ended December 31, 2025, to $13.1

billion, or 80% of total revenue, for the six months ended June 30, 2026. Subscription-based revenue increased

from $98 million, or 25% of total revenue, to $789 million, or 17% of total revenue, to $3.2 billion, or 20% of

total revenue, over the same periods. The higher increase in consumption-based revenue reflects the growth in

customers with $100,000 or more of revenue on a TTM basis. For 2023, our revenue of $41 million was

substantially all consumption-based, reflecting our early operations. We expect consumption-based revenue to

continue to represent the substantial majority of total revenue for the foreseeable future.

Our recent growth may reflect early-stage adoption dynamics, the nascency of the AI market, and rapid initial

customer expansion that may not be sustainable over time. We expect our revenue growth rate to decline over

time, and it may decline significantly and more rapidly than we anticipate as our revenue base increases and as

the market matures.

Our Customers

We serve enterprises of all sizes and across industries, in nearly every region of the world. Organizations that

use Claude range from the worlds largest enterprises to AI-native startups, research institutions, and mission-

driven organizations working on some of the worlds most consequential problems. Customers realize value

from Claude in three primary ways more productive employees, faster processes, and embedded intelligence in

their products. As enterprise customers push Claude into increasingly complex workflows, we engage with them

to understand their evolving requirements, which helps shape our product roadmap. This feedback loop

deepens customer relationships with each release and makes Claude more capable for all customers.

Software developers have been core to our strategy they are simultaneously the architects of applications built

on our platform, and they encode their workflows and institutional knowledge into Claude-powered agents.

Developers have been our earliest adopters and often most discerning users they apply models to unanticipated

use cases quickly and surface the edge cases that sharpen each successive version of a Claude model. Claude

Code is now embedded in software development workflows across major organizations, with these relationships

often beginning with individual engineers who recognized the value of autonomous coding agents and

accelerated adoption across their enterprises.

The same intelligence and capabilities we deliver to enterprises are also valuable to prosumers in their personal

endeavors. These users provide strategic value as a top-of-funnel engine for our enterprise business

individuals who utilize Claude for their personal work and then champion it inside their organizations.

We are seeing broad-based growth across customer use cases, from software development to financial analysis,

content creation, and complex knowledge work. Claude is extensible regardless of entry point, enterprises

quickly discover that frontier AI applies to a wide and expanding range of tasks across their organizations. As a

result, what sometimes begins as a single use case frequently expands into broader adoption spanning coding,

legal analysis, financial modeling, customer operations, and other knowledge-intensive work.

Our Approach to Claude Diffusion

A core driver of Claudes diffusion is its intelligenceas Claudes capabilities advance, value to customers

expands, and that value drives greater adoption. Ensuring the accessibility of such intelligence is also critical,

and we do that in the following ways through our composable platform and applications that meet customers

our reach at scale.

Intelligence-Led Growth. Our platform and applications are the primary mechanism through which Claudes

organizations operate. This pattern repeats across organizations prosumers become internal champions, teams

expand their use of Claude, and what begins as a single workflow grows into something that impacts how an

entire organization operates. This pattern has been core to our revenue growth.

Direct Teams. This organic adoption is complemented and enhanced through our direct go-to-market teams

who help scale Claude to organizations with complex workflows and business processes. By the time our direct

teams engage a customer, Claude is often already in use. Our go-to-market teams use this organic momentum

Applied AI Teams. Our Applied AI teams embed more deeply with select customers, building solutions that

make it easier for organizations to benefit from Claudes intelligence. They work with enterprises to design and

deploy custom solutions, specialized workflows, and domain-specific implementations tailored to the

Key Factors Affecting Our Performance

Remaining at the Frontier of AI

We believe enterprises consistently choose the most capable and trustworthy AI systems, making our position

at the frontier an important driver of our success. Our ability to rapidly grow our revenue is partially dependent

on our continued delivery of more intelligent and capable models. Our experience is that as we deliver models

with meaningfully improved capabilities that deliver higher intelligence per dollar spent, customers find new

applications for AI across their businesses, accelerating adoption and revenue growth.

This can be seen in the trajectory of our model family adoption. Successive versions of our most intelligent

generally available model have delivered meaningfully greater intelligence and capabilities. With each release,

we have seen most customers utilize the latest version of our models, reinforcing the direct relationship

between frontier model quality and revenue generation. As we deliver more capable models, customers have

expanded their usage into new use cases and increased their spending accordingly.

We expect our ability to deliver innovation in intelligence and capability at the frontier to remain a key driver of

revenue growth in future periods, and maintaining a leading position requires continued investment in research

and compute, top technical talent, and access to capital sufficient to support our growth trajectory.

Securing Sufficient Compute and Optimizing its Utilization

Our ability to continue advancing intelligence at the frontier depends on securing sufficient compute for

research, model development, serving customers, and internal usage of Claude. Our ability to leverage

heterogeneous compute helps us to secure compute at sufficient scale and optimal price-performance. If any of

our chip partners are unable to deliver committed compute or experience delays, or if we need to procure

incremental compute, we benefit from having a range of partners as opposed to being overly reliant on any one

partner. The availability, timing, and cost of compute are subject to factors largely outside our control,

including the concentration of chip and memory manufacturing among a limited number of suppliers and

geographies, constraints on the availability of power and data center capacity, and evolving export controls. We

have historically operated in a compute-constrained environment in which demand for compute has exceeded

the supply available to us. In addition, certain of the CSPs on which we rely for compute are also strategic

investors in us, are our customers, and offer AI models and services that compete with ours. These multifaceted

relationships could give rise to conflicts of interest or competitive dynamics that adversely affect our access to

compute, its cost, or the prioritization of our workloads.

Claude adoption is broad-based. No single customer represented more than 10% of our revenue for the nine

months ended September 30, 2026. Approximately 79% of our revenue came from customers outside of our top

five customers for the three months ended September 30, 2026, up from approximately 64% for the three

months ended March 31, 2026. At the same time, our largest customers continue to expand. For the three

months ended September 30, 2026, our revenue from each of our top five customers reached a record high.

Customer Usage Patterns

Our revenue is influenced by customer usage patterns, which are impacted by the frequency and capability of

new model version releases, as well as seasonal trends, each of which can create variability in our results from

period to period. The continuous and overlapping cadence of new model version releases is inherent to

remaining at the frontier of AI development. For example, model versions released in the six months ended

September 30, 2026 accounted for over 80% of our revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026.

As we have released successive versions of Claude models, we have continued to see revenue growth as new

capabilities unlock additional use cases, prompting new customers to adopt Claude and existing customers to

expand their usage.

Our customer usage patterns have also typically exhibited seasonality, particularly around holiday and vacation

periods in the summer and winter, when certain workflows slow down and overall customer activity declines.

For example, developer commits and feature launches are typically slower during the code freezes that many

companies schedule over holidays. Consistent with this pattern, for the two weeks ended January 2, 2026, total

customer spend declined by approximately 20% compared to the prior two weeks ended December 19, 2025.

However, our historical growth rates may mask the impact of seasonal trends on our results. These dynamics

are inherent aspects of a pay-for-intelligence revenue model and may be important determinants of our

financial results in future periods.

Because of these seasonal patterns, our results of operations have varied, and are expected to continue to vary,

from period to period, and our results for any one period may not be indicative of our results for any future

period. As our revenue growth rate declines over time, we expect seasonal effects to become more apparent in,

and to have a greater effect on, our period-to-period results, and to make it more difficult to forecast our results

of operations.

Components of Results of Operations

Revenue

We generate revenue as customers use Claude through our platform and applications, including Claude Code,

Claude Cowork, and vertical solutions for key industries. Claude is powered by our proprietary Claude model

family, a set of frontier models, each with varying levels of capability, cost, and speed. Enterprise customers

access our intelligence service directly from us or through CSP marketplaces, where we maintain direct

contractual relationships with our customers.

Depending on the model, platform, or applications chosen by our customers, they enter into pay-for-

intelligence pricing, subscription-based pricing, or both. A growing number of enterprise customers agree to

annual or multi-year contracts with minimum spend commitments.

Revenue is recognized when customers use our intelligence service, based on the quantity of tokens consumed.

Subscriptions are generally prepaid on a monthly or annual basis, and are subject to usage limits that reset over

short periods, which results in revenue being recognized on a ratable basis over the subscription period, which

is generally 12 months or less.

We expect our revenue growth rate to decline over time and to fluctuate from period to period. Revenue growth

in any period may be affected by customer usage patterns, customer adoption, the deployment of our models,

platform, and applications, the pricing of our offerings, the timing and success of new model and product

releases, measures we decide to implement to safely deploy new capabilities, our ability to continue meeting

customer requirements, and other factors affecting demand for our models, platform, and applications,

including those described in the section titled Risk FactorsWe are at an early stage of our revenue growth, we

Operating Expenses

Compute and Infrastructure

Compute and infrastructure expenses primarily consist of costs attributable to operating our technology

infrastructure, which supports the full range of workloads for our models, including research, model

development, serving customers, and internal usage of Claude. These costs primarily consist of compute fees

paid to CSPs under committed and on-demand arrangements, including storage, networking, and connectivity,

and related support costs, infrastructure costs associated with serving customer workloads through partner

channels, colocation facility costs, equipment leases and committed power costs associated with our data center

operations, and third-party application infrastructure software costs directly related to our compute operations.

Beginning in 2026, as our obligations commence under compute and infrastructure leases, these expenses also

include operating lease costs, amortization of finance lease assets, variable lease costs, and depreciation and

amortization on owned servers, networking equipment, and related data center assets. See Note 8 to our

consolidated financial statements included elsewhere in this prospectus for a functional presentation of our

operating expenses, including cost of revenue, and a description of the workloads underlying the compute and

infrastructure expenses within each functional caption. Within that functional presentation, substantially all of

the cost of revenue consists of compute and infrastructure expenses associated with customer-serving

workloads. Additionally, cost of revenue includes certain personnel-related costs and allocated overhead costs.

Customers adopt and retain our technology based on whether our models are at the frontier of AI capability and

maintaining that frontier position is essential to revenue generation. Each model release represents continuous

intelligence improvements, making ongoing model development a critical factor in revenue generation. We

operate an iterative cycle in which compute deployed to model development yields more capable and efficient

models. Those efficiency gains free compute to serve additional customer workloads. In turn, serving our

customers at scale generates feedback that informs research and model development. In this cycle, customer-

serving activity and model development are each a direct input that informs the other, and we therefore view

compute and infrastructure costs as critical to our ability to generate revenue within a short time of incurring

such costs.

We expect compute and infrastructure expenses to increase in absolute dollars and remain our largest

operating expense for the foreseeable future as additional committed compute becomes available to us under

multi-year arrangements with CSPs, and as we continue to invest in additional compute. These expenses are

paid and recognized as compute becomes available to us, rather than when committed, and are generally fixed

in the near term given the majority of our compute is contracted under multi-year commitments that we

typically procure 12 to 18 months in advance of availability. We deploy this committed compute as a single,

shared resource across all workloads, and thus plan, incur, and assess it on a total basis rather than by

individual workload. As a result, compute and infrastructure expenses as a percentage of revenue will depend

primarily on how our revenue scales relative to our total committed compute cost base. In addition, as part of

our public benefit initiatives, we intend to direct a meaningful portion of our resources, which may include

compute, toward ensuring that the benefits of frontier AI are broadly accessible, and we expect to significantly

scale these efforts over time.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures

To supplement our consolidated financial statements prepared and presented in accordance with GAAP, we use

certain non-GAAP financial measures, as described below, to facilitate analysis of our operating performance

and for internal planning and forecasting purposes.

We use Adjusted Operating Income (Loss), Adjusted Operating Margin, and Adjusted Net Income (Loss) to

evaluate our operating performance, formulate business plans, prepare budgets and forecasts, and make

strategic decisions, including those relating to operating expenses and the allocation of internal resources. By

excluding certain items that management believes are not directly reflective of the ongoing operating

performance of our business, we believe these non-GAAP financial measures provide investors, analysts, and

others with meaningful insight and enhance their overall understanding of our business and financial

performance by providing a more consistent and comparable overview of our financial performance across

periods.

Other companies in our industry, including those with similar business models, may define or calculate

similarly titled measures in different ways, which may limit the usefulness of these measures for direct

comparison. In addition, other companies may not publish these or similar metrics. Further, these metrics have

certain limitations in that they do not include the impact of certain expenses that are reflected on our

consolidated statements of operations. Thus, our non-GAAP financial measures should not be viewed in

isolation or as a replacement for the most directly comparable measures prepared in accordance with GAAP.

We encourage investors and others to review our business, financial condition, and results of operations in their

entirety, not to rely on any single financial measure, and to view our non-GAAP financial measures in

conjunction with their respective most directly comparable financial measures calculated in accordance with

GAAP, as well as our consolidated financial statements and accompanying notes included elsewhere in this

prospectus.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Since inception, we have financed our operations, corporate investments, and capital expenditures primarily

through proceeds from the issuance of convertible promissory notes, redeemable convertible preferred stock,

cash generated from sales, and SAFEs. To date, we have financed our compute and infrastructure primarily

under arrangements with our CSPs, as well as through proceeds from the financing activities described above

and cash generated from operations. As of June 30, 2026, we had cash and cash equivalents of $112.4 billion,

$19.3 billion of short-term investments, and $3.2 billion available under the Credit Facility, as described below.

Cash and cash equivalents consist of cash held in checking and interest-bearing accounts, money market funds,

and other highly liquid investments with original maturities of three months or less from the date of purchase.

We have generated losses from our operations, as reflected in our accumulated deficit of $132.9 billion, which

includes $113.2 billion related to changes in the fair value of our convertible notes driven by increases in our

enterprise valuation and derivative liabilities since 2023, including $72.4 billion during the six months ended

June30, 2026. Additionally, we have generated negative cash flows from operating and investing activities in

2023, 2024, and 2025, driven by ongoing expenditures to support business growth including investments to

secure access to more compute and infrastructure capacity. We continue to invest in our own data center

infrastructure and enter into compute and infrastructure capacity arrangements to support anticipated demand

for our intelligence service. Given the long lead times between contracting for, purchasing, or leasing compute

and that compute becoming available to serve workloads, we secure these arrangements well in advance of

anticipated demand. To secure certain data center development projects, which require significant capital

commitments in advance of deployment, we have in some cases relied, and expect to continue to rely, on

arrangements with financing sources or partners who guarantee the lease obligations to support the

development of data center capacity that is expected to host such partners chips or computing infrastructure.

Our ability to expand compute and infrastructure capacity through these arrangements in the future may

depend on the continued willingness and ability of a limited number of CSPs, partners, and financing sources to

enter into and perform under such arrangements. In some cases, we may also make payments in advance of the

delivery of the equipment or infrastructure. We anticipate making significant and increased investments for the

foreseeable future, including for owned or leased compute and infrastructure in advance of delivery. These

investments help ensure we have the technological resources to maintain and expand our position at the

frontier of AI.

Our increasing efforts to build out our compute and infrastructure require substantial capital expenditures and

are changing the cash flow profile of our business. Whereas the compute purchased through our CSP

arrangements is generally paid as the compute becomes available to us, we expect an increasing portion of our

compute and infrastructure investment to take the form of capital expenditures and other commitments,

including lease obligations, that require significant upfront payments well in advance of the related compute

becoming operational. We expect our capital expenditures to increase significantly as we expand our build-out,

and we expect these investments to result in significant net cash used in operating and investing activities for

the foreseeable future. Our net cash used in these activities may increase regardless of whether our financial

performance improves. The magnitude and timing of these investments are inherently uncertain and may

Revolving Credit Agreement

In May 2025, we entered into a Credit Agreement (as amended from time to time prior to the AR Credit

Agreement (as defined below), the Credit Agreement) with certain lenders from time to time party thereto

and Morgan Stanley Senior Funding, Inc., as administrative agent and collateral agent for the lenders, which

initially provided for a $2.5 billion senior secured revolving credit facility with a $400 million letter of credit

sublimit (the Initial Credit Facility). In September 2025, we amended the Initial Credit Facility to increase the

aggregate revolving commitments to $2.75 billion and in November 2025, we further amended the Initial Credit

Facility to increase the aggregate revolving commitments to $3.25 billion (the Initial Credit Facility, as

amended, the Credit Facility). As of June30, 2026, we had no borrowings drawn under the Credit Facility and

$78 million of standby letters of credit outstanding. As of June30, 2026, we were in compliance with all

covenants under the Credit Agreement.

In September 2026, we entered into an Amended and Restated Credit Agreement (the AR Credit

Agreement), which amended and restated the Credit Agreement to provide for a $15 billion senior secured

revolving credit facility with the full amount thereof available for letters of credit (the AR Credit Facility).

The AR Credit Facility will mature on September 4, 2031, subject to earlier springing maturity if more than

$500 million in aggregate scheduled cash principal payments on certain of our indebtedness are required

within the 91-day period preceding the date on which any such scheduled principal payment is due, and we do

not have sufficient liquidity to make all such payments then due and remain in pro forma compliance with our

financial covenant, subject to certain other exceptions and conditions set forth in the AR Credit Agreement.

The proceeds of the AR Credit Facility may be used for working capital, general corporate purposes, and other

lawful purposes. We may request borrowings in U.S. dollars or certain other agreed currencies, subject to the

terms of the AR Credit Agreement.
